Bali Belly vs Food Poisoning: What Is the Difference?


Are they the same thing?

Not exactly, but they overlap heavily. "Bali Belly" is a traveller's nickname for diarrhoea and stomach upset during a stay in Bali, and doctors usually call it traveller's diarrhoea. "Food poisoning" describes illness caused by eating contaminated food. Many cases of Bali Belly are caused by contaminated food or water, so in practice the two often describe the same event.

How they can differ

Timing. Some food poisoning, particularly from toxins already present in the food, can start within hours of a meal and settle within a day or two. Traveller's diarrhoea caused by bacteria, viruses or parasites often starts later, sometimes a day or more after exposure, and can last longer.

Main symptoms. Early, heavy vomiting is more typical of toxin-type food poisoning. Watery diarrhoea with cramps is the classic picture of traveller's diarrhoea. Both can cause nausea, tiredness and a low fever.

Who else is ill. If several people who ate the same meal become ill around the same time, food poisoning from that meal is more likely.

You cannot reliably tell the cause from symptoms alone, and it usually does not change the first steps of care.

What to do for either

  • Replace fluids with small, frequent sips of water or oral rehydration solution.
  • Rest and avoid alcohol.
  • Eat plain food only when your appetite returns.
  • Ask a pharmacist or doctor before taking anti-diarrhoeal medicine, especially with fever or blood in the stool.
  • Wash your hands carefully and avoid preparing food for others while you are unwell.

When to see a doctor

Get assessed if you have blood in your stool or vomit, a high fever, severe or worsening abdominal pain, signs of dehydration, vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down, or symptoms that are not improving after a few days. Infants, older adults and pregnant women should be assessed early. Go to the nearest emergency room for fainting, confusion, or inability to keep any fluids down.
