Bali Belly and IV Drips: Do You Actually Need One?


Not everyone needs an IV drip

Bali Belly causes fluid and salt loss through diarrhoea and vomiting. In mild cases, drinking small, frequent amounts of water or oral rehydration solution is often enough, and an IV drip is not needed. Whether an IV is appropriate is a medical decision made after an assessment.

When oral fluids are usually enough

  • You can keep fluids down
  • You are passing urine regularly and it is not very dark
  • You are not dizzy when you stand up
  • Symptoms are mild and starting to improve

When a doctor may recommend IV fluids

A doctor may consider an IV drip when dehydration is moderate or worse, or when oral rehydration is not working, for example if you are vomiting so much that you cannot keep fluids down. The decision depends on your symptoms, how long you have been ill, your hydration, your medical history and the examination findings.

What an IV drip does and does not do

An IV drip gives fluids and electrolytes directly into the bloodstream, which can correct dehydration faster than drinking when you cannot keep fluids down. It does not cure the infection itself. Medication such as anti-nausea treatment may be given alongside it if the doctor judges it appropriate.

Signs you should be assessed urgently

Go to the nearest emergency room for fainting, confusion, blood in stool or vomit, severe or worsening abdominal pain, very high fever, or inability to keep any fluids down. A home visit is not a substitute for emergency care.

What a home visit involves

Our doctor assesses you at your villa or hotel and decides what care is appropriate. That may be oral fluids and advice, medication, or an IV drip, and some patients are advised on oral fluids and other care instead.
