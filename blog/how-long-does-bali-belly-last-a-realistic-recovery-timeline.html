How Long Does Bali Belly Last? A Realistic Recovery Timeline

The short answer

Most cases of Bali Belly, which doctors usually call traveller's diarrhoea, improve within a few days, and most people feel back to normal within about a week. How long yours lasts depends on the cause, how much fluid you can keep down, and your general health.

A typical timeline

Day 1 to 2: Symptoms often start suddenly with loose stools, stomach cramps, nausea, and sometimes vomiting or a mild fever. This is usually the roughest stretch.

Day 2 to 4: Vomiting commonly settles first. Stools may stay loose, and tiredness and low appetite can linger.

Day 4 to 7: Most people are clearly improving, although the stomach can stay sensitive for a few more days.

This is a general guide, not a promise. Some infections run longer, and some people recover faster.

What helps you recover

  • Drink small, frequent sips of water or oral rehydration solution. Oral rehydration salts are sold in pharmacies in Bali.
  • Rest, and return to plain food such as rice, bread or bananas once your appetite comes back.
  • Avoid alcohol, and avoid very oily or spicy food until you feel better.
  • Ask a pharmacist or doctor before taking anti-diarrhoeal medicine, especially if you have a fever or blood in your stool.
  • Do not start antibiotics on your own. They are not needed in every case, and the doctor decides whether they are appropriate.

    • Signs you should not wait it out

    Get a medical assessment if you notice any of the following:

  • You cannot keep fluids down

  • Blood in your stool or vomit

  • Severe or worsening abdominal pain

  • High fever, confusion or fainting

  • Signs of dehydration: dizziness when standing, very dark or very little urine, a dry mouth

  • Symptoms that are not improving after a few days

  • Diarrhoea that lasts more than a week or two, because some parasitic infections need specific treatment


Infants, older adults, pregnant women and people with other medical conditions should be assessed early. For fainting, confusion, blood in vomit, or inability to keep any fluids down, go to the nearest emergency room.

If you are too unwell to travel to a clinic

If you are stable but too unwell to go out, a doctor can assess you at your villa or hotel. Read how a doctor home visit works, check the areas we cover, or review the full list of symptoms and warning signs.
