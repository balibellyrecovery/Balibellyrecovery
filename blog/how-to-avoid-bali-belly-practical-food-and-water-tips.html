How to Avoid Bali Belly: Practical Food and Water Tips

You can lower the risk, not remove it

Even careful travellers get Bali Belly sometimes. The tips below reduce your exposure to the contaminated food and water that cause most cases.

Water

  • Drink sealed bottled water or water that has been properly treated.
  • Use the same water for brushing your teeth.
  • Be cautious with ice when you are unsure where it came from. If in doubt, ask or skip it.
  • Keep your mouth closed in the shower and when swimming in pools or the sea where water quality is unknown.

    • Food

  • Choose food that is freshly cooked and served hot.

  • Be careful with raw or undercooked meat, seafood and eggs.

  • Be cautious with salads and raw vegetables. Fruit you peel yourself is generally safer.

  • Avoid food that has been sitting out at warm temperatures, such as buffets or street stalls without turnover.

  • A busy place with fast turnover is usually a better sign than an empty one, but it is not a guarantee.

    • Hygiene

  • Wash your hands with soap and water before eating, and after using the toilet.

  • Carry hand sanitiser for times when soap is not available.

  • Do not share utensils or drinks when someone in your group is unwell.


Before you travel

Ask a travel doctor or clinic about vaccinations that may be relevant to your trip, such as typhoid and hepatitis A, and check that your travel insurance covers medical care.

If you still get sick

Rest, sip fluids or oral rehydration solution often, and avoid alcohol. See a doctor for a high fever, blood in your stool, severe or worsening abdominal pain, signs of dehydration, or if you cannot keep fluids down. Learn the warning signs, or see how a doctor home visit works if you are too unwell to travel.

Important note

This article is general information and does not replace an individual medical assessment.
